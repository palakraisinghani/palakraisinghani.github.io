This is an account of my trip to Japan earlier in May 2025. The poem below captures my impression of the Japanese lifestyle, as I used the local transport to commute. Following which I share how the trip was awakening for me. Japanese culture is an inspiration for good lifestyle and discipline, and this post will serve as a reminder of that inspiration to me, and to others I hope you have a nice read and I would wish that you plan a trip to Japan sometime.

Busy railway stations,
Looking for boards of hues different.
Matching patterns to find my way
And with some helping hands lent.

Busy road crossings,
The busiest I have ever seen.
In those bidirectional currents of people,
Memories fresh as yestreen.

My trip to Japan was extremely enlightening. I understood the importance of culture and lifestyle. How simple cultural differences can carve a very different trajectory. For example, in Japan, the aged people, continue to stay active. They do household chores and don’t let age catch up. The Japanese culture has great regard for timeliness and etiquette in various social circles. Routine habits are inculcated in children at a very young age. Routine and etiquette are impressed on these young minds and it reflects in all aspects of their life.

Japanese philosophy has become more accessible to the world with more and more books being written, most notable book being Ikigai. Their philosophy is deeply intertwined in their art and lifestyle and its effect can be seen in various aspects of their life. Simplicity and minimalism in Ikebana - the Japanese art flower arrangement reflects in the choice of furnishing and dressing. On one occasion, when I shared my ambitious travel plan with a Japanese lady, she told us to just visit a few places and to do them well. They aim for perfection in all activities they engage in. As a foreigner you will witness cleanliness of the cities, timeliness of all services and sincere efforts in hospitality. What seems like a quite simple dedicated country in the day time strikes a different chord at night. You might almost feel like you have transitioned into anime land. People dressed in costumes, brightly coloured banners and hoardings and a buzz from people engaging in conversation and making their way through crowd. In Expo 25 which was being organized while I was there I saw 90-year olds in wheel chairs living their mascot obsession. It blew my mind to be able to reason why people purchase these items. The pattern becomes more clear in Disneyland and even more on the Tokyo city streets at night. This piece of the Japanese lifestyle felt missing.

On our flight back to India, we met a Japanese man who had settled in Mumbai and we asked him how his experience in India was and why he had chosen to leave his home country to settle in another so different in lifestyle. He said he liked the daily dose of surprises and the randomness more than a timely routine and perfection. He said he found Japan boring to stay in over the years. It is not right to generalize his view but it does partially answer our question and fills in the missing piece explaining why the streets get so lively and abuzz at night.

Another aspect of the Japanese lifestyle that is very admirable and distinctive is their cuisine. Ultra and super- processed foods have little space and fresh organic simple foods with less preservatives dominate their food market. I was pleasantly surprised when I found out that Dorayaki has sweet red bean as filling. You can tell their street food is very organic. A simple Japanese meal is very wholesome and full of nutrition. Unlike western countries, where bread dominates the meal, in Japan, a variety of salads are included in the meal. This is not very surprising when you consider that they mostly have rice for grains and they use chopsticks to consume it, which by design, reduces the intake of grains and replaces it with other more nutritional options.

Overall, trip was great and I wrote this blog mainly to serve as inspiration to live a healthy and active lifestyle and live responsibly in society following the necessary etiquettes.
