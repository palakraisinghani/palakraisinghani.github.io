The Tale that tells Taste

May 22, 2026 Philosophy, Reflections

How many times did you go to new place to eat and were excited to try something new, but were afraid you won’t like it? You may look around to see if it’s a popular item or if you can spot just one person who has bought the same item. The food on their might look great but it still doesn’t tell you how it tastes. Of course, the look matters but taste matters more. ( footnote : you can’t eat food that tastes bad no matter how good it looks.) Then you look at the person eating it and notice their expressions. Are they pleasant? Is the food spicy, sweet or tangy? Often this gives you enough information to decide whether to have the dish or not. If you have done this, you will know it works well.

I did the same thing recently except that this time I had watched people on an advertisement really enjoy a chocolate and really wanting the same experience I went and bought that chocolate. I took the first bite, but to my disappointment I didn’t really like it. Then some days later I had it again with a friend. This time it didn’t taste that bad. Well, the taste had obviously not changed. What did change was my expectations from the chocolate. If I were to compare my satisfaction of having the chocolate for the first time with really high expectations with normal everyday lunch, I would rate my regular lunch higher. The point here is that the burden of expectations often drains joy and satisfaction. At a greater scale this applies to life too. Imagine having a new food item for which you have no sources of information. Or randomly picking a new street to walk on. Even the simplest stores, or a street cat may come by as a pleasant surprise. You may run into a new brand that becomes your favorite or a cool dish that instantly goes on an Instagram post. The possibilities are many. The mantra to be happy is to act without expectations. Without pre-defining result guardrails for yourself. Now there are better ways to express this idea of not acting simply out of expectation of the outcome. They say enjoy the journey don’t just chase the destination. I chose this example to show how easy it is to fall into the expectation trap, and how often we probably fall for it, often unaware.

As a student, I remind myself to not only study for marks but more importantly for learning. We don’t do research to publish a paper, but to learn and to improve the state-of-the-art in the field we are working in. We write not to impress but to convey our ideas to the world. We read not to check a list but better our understanding and awareness with every piece of literature consumed or its part.
