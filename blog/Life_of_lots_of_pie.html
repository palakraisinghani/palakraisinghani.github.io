This is the tale of my JEE Journey, but I did have PIE! It was a really fun time, I made loads of friends and here I share the most valuable insights for this extremely important time in your life as a science/tech student in India.

“What did your JEE preparation journey look like?” – a question that echoes in every city for weeks after the JEE results come out. We often learn from other people. Their mistakes and their tricks. Juniors ask seniors, who intern had asked their seniors and so forth. I had done the same. I asked people, my parents also asked people. The same question – “How did you prepare? what books did you use? where did you study? When did you sleep and for how long? Did you leave everything else? What did your monthly assessment look like?”. Back in my time when I listened to answers, I would immediately set benchmarks for myself. I must do so and so since so and so did so and so and so on. But everybody has a different success story. I can’t do all of that. I possibly can’t do good and bad in the same test. Everybody has a different learning curve. A different strategy. I had my own story at the end of 11th and 12th and it didn’t match any I had heard before from my seniors. It was yet another story. A rather imperfect one.

Very close to JEE Mains, my performance in maths suddenly dropped. It was November and I had two months only. I didn’t lose hope and took very focused and directed steps to improve my performance and reduce silly mistakes. So close to the exams, I could have easily given up. Twists can come anytime, but can you stay calm and grounded? Can you have the faith that with some disciple and patience you can make it through.

I remember one of our teachers would say, to clear the exam, solve as many questions as you can but if nothing else at least minimally solve previous 46 years’ papers. When I had started my preparation, I would at thins thinking I would do much more. Much to my own surprise, at the end of 12th I still hadn’t solved the previous year papers. I still made it through the exam. I had obviously done a lot of other things. What I am saying is that there is no such thing as a perfect preparation. Of course, you got to set high benchmarks for yourself and keep chasing them. Don’t forget that there are so many people who give the exam, and they form a complete spectrum and your consistency, discipline and determination to not give up no matter what decides your story and position in the spectrum. Make your own story. Do what feels right and suits you. Follow your heart.

Follow what strategy you want but remember this - there is no shortcut to hard work. Do not find shortcuts to understanding. You got to understand things well. If it’s hard to understand something, spend more time on it. Understand it as well as you can. I can’t emphasize this enough. The amount of syllabus only grows with time and by the time you realize the weight of taking the shortcut, it might already be late, and a complete repair nearly impossible.

Lastly and most importantly, I would like to share something I heard more recently. Motivation is a feeling and feelings change. They never stay constant. You can’t stay motivated constantly for two years. But you can stay disciplined for two years. Don’t go by motivation because that is the result of discipline and determination. It is your action – in the form of discipline that you should work on. If you can stay disciplined, you will naturally be motivated. You will have the confidence that you rise after a local minimum. It’s your mindset that matters. Your mental strength to be disciplined with study even when your performance is lower than your normal.

I hope this blog is insightful. I have tried my best to deliver very concentrated and well filtered advice, learning from my own experience and that of others too.
